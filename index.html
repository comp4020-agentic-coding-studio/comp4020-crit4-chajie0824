Drag across a few bells for a glissando. Striking dead-center rings the "center tone" (正鼓音, zhènggǔyīn); off to the side rings the "edge tone" (侧鼓音, cègǔyīn) — a minor third apart. That's genuinely how a real bianzhong works: one bell, two pitches. The note names shown pair the Chinese scale-degree name with its rough do-re-mi-fa-sol-la-ti equivalent — they aren't an exact translation of each other, just the closest match.

Keys Q W E R T Y U I O P A S D F G H J play each bell low to high. The smaller row above is a niuzhong (钮钟) tier — display only, it doesn't sound. Pick a tune, then "Play" to hear its intro once, or "Follow Along" to have a ring mark the next bell to strike — the letter shown is the key to press.
